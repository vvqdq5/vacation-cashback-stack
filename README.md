## Why bundles win, and the comparison mistake people keep making

Bundled packages are cheaper because travel companies negotiate bulk rates individual travelers can't access, then pass part of the margin on to fill seats and rooms. The documented saving is 15–20% versus booking separately.

The common error is comparing hotel nightly rates in isolation. A $150/night hotel plus a $400 flight looks cheaper than a $200/night package until you realize the package already contains the airfare. Las Vegas is the clearest example: $312 for three nights plus roundtrip airfare beats almost any à la carte combination from most US cities.

Timing matters as much as bundling. Off-season and shoulder-season travel drops package prices 30–50%. The consistently cheapest booking windows run January through early March, mid-October through early December, and late April through mid-May. All-inclusive packages in Mexico and the Caribbean typically run $600–$1,200 per person during their own low season (roughly May–June and September–November), versus noticeably more in winter. The two windows don't overlap perfectly, so check prices for your specific destination and dates rather than assuming one calendar fits all.
